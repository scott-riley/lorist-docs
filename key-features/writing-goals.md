# Writing goals

You can set weekly word goals or per–document word count goals on any folder in your Library.

## Weekly word goals

Weekly word goals are useful for anyone who wants to write consistently over time. They’re a simple but notable way to track your progress towards any big projects, like a book or a thesis, or any projects that see you writing pieces consistently, such as blogs or newsletters.

<img src="/weekly-goals.webp" alt="Weekly goal readout in a Lorist folder" />

To view your progress towards your weekly word goals, navigate to any folder (either in the sidebar or through <a href="/key-features/go-to/">quick navigation</a>) that has weekly goals enabled and you’ll see your current progress.

## Per–document word goals

Per–document word goals let you set a target word count for any documents within a specific folder. They’re useful for any project where you need to hit a word count target for any piece you write, such as chapters of a book, or blog posts of a consistent length.

<img src="/daily-goals.png" alt="Per–document goal readout in a Lorist editor menu" />

You’ll see a circular progress indicator in the top right of your editor whenever you’re editing a document in a folder with per–document word goals set.

## Configuring goals

To set either weekly or per–document goals (or both!), hover over any folder in the sidebar, hit the More button, and select ‘Set goals’ from the popover menu. If your folder already has at least one type of goal set, this will read ‘Edit goals’.

<img src="/folder-options.webp" alt="The folder menu in Lorist" />

From the resulting popover, you can enable per–document and weekly word goals individually, and set a target for each.

<img src="/editing-goals.webp" alt="Setting goals in Lorist" />

Hit save once you’re happy with your _achievable and sane_ goals and your folders and documents will start showing the appropriate tracking for your goals.

## Recommendations for goals

Some people respond really well to ambitious word count targets, and others absolutely hate them. Many folks sit somewhere in the middle, where _some_ tracking or accountability helps, as long as it doesn’t become excessive or obsessive.

If you don’t feel like you _need_ word goals then _don’t set any_. There’s absolutely no reason to put pressure on yourself to write, and it’s very easy to ‘game’ any kind of output-specific goals, especially for something like writing.

If you do find you respond well to the visibility and accountability goals can give, then consider starting with **achievable** goals. To put it into perspective, the range of _daily_ words that _actual professional authors_ write is as spread-out as 500–3,000 words, according to <a href="https://authorlytica.com/daily-word-counts-of-authors/" target="_blank">Authorlytica</a>. That’s 3,500–21,000 words per week. If you’re setting word goals _anywhere near_ the top end of that, and you’re not a professional, prolific author, then consider taking a step back.

Don’t tie your quantity or consistency to your self–worth, either. If setting writing goals motivates you to write consistently, and write _well_ more often than not, then great. If they encourage you to write for the sake of writing, and you _don’t want_ to write for the sake of writing; then turn them off.

Also, _Ulysses_ took seven years to write, and no one’s calling Joyce a bad author.
